The Problem

Most staffing decisions get made on gut feel.

Soonest is a healthcare staff scheduling platform built to take the guesswork out of staffing. Today, most groups staff to what they ran last month, or what feels about right. Almost nobody sets a target for what a given volume requires, and almost nobody measures against it afterward. That's where the money goes: a little every day, at every location, with nobody tracking it.

Where the savings come from

Distribution

Most groups staff about the same every day, even though a Monday and a Thursday are not the same. You pay for hours you didn't need on light days and overtime on heavy ones. Same headcount, wrong distribution. It costs you twice.

Mix

When most of your team is full time, every surge becomes overtime because there's nobody left to call. The right ratio of part time and PRN staff covers the same shift at straight time.

This is not about cutting staff. It's about staffing to your actual volume with the right mix of people. Overstaffed days drain the budget. Understaffed days fall on the same few people who always cover, and that's how burnout starts to build.

The Basics

What is labor forecasting in healthcare?

Labor forecasting predicts patient volume by day and location, then calculates exactly how many staff of each role that volume requires. It gives every clinic a target: hours per visit and cost per visit. You schedule against that target, then measure what actually happened.

Labor forecasting software keeps that loop running automatically, so the forecast, the roster, and the schedule all read from the same numbers.

Most tools tell you who is working.
This tells you how many people should be working in the first place.

The Staffing Grid

At X visits per day, how many of each role do you need?

The grid is one standardized model you can apply across every location. Volume levels run down the rows, roles run across the columns. Smaller clinics land on higher targets from the same grid, and that's expected.

  • Variable roles flex with volume: MAs, techs, front desk
  • Fixed roles stay put: practice managers and admin
  • Weighted by how often each clinic actually sits at each volume level
  • Rolls up to paid hours per visit and cost per visit
